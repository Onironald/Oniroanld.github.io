01

Retail Sales Analysis

Completed 2 August 2026

Excel • Pivot Tables • Dashboard • Sales Analysis • Data Visualization

Project Type

Individual analytics project completed by Chidi Onochie Ronald.

Business Objective

The project analyzed retail sales performance across three branches and five product categories to understand revenue, customer behaviour, product performance and unusual transactions.

Data & Approach

The dataset contained 1,000 retail transactions. The analysis covered data cleaning, data exploration, outlier analysis using the IQR method, revenue analysis, branch comparison, customer/member analysis and correlation analysis.

Understanding the Dataset

What this data represents: This is a transaction-level retail sales dataset. The analysis contains 1,000 actual sales transactions across 3 branches and 5 product categories. The source presentation describes the file as 1,001 rows including the header and 13 columns.

How to read one row: Each row represents a retail purchase record. It combines information about the branch, product, customer type, gender, quantity purchased and the value of the transaction. That means the dataset lets us move from individual transactions to branch, product and customer-level performance.

Where?

Branch A — New York; Branch B — Los Angeles; Branch C — Chicago.

What was sold?

Products grouped into five categories: Beverages, Fruits, Household, Personal Care and Stationery.

Who bought?

The dataset includes customer type (Member/Normal) and gender, allowing basic customer segmentation.

What we checked before analysing

  • No missing values were found across the 1,000 records.
  • Unit Price, Quantity and Total Price were validated as numeric fields.
  • Duplicate records were checked and none were found.
  • Branch/city labels were standardised, including correcting Chicago entries to Branch C.
  • XLOOKUP was used to validate product-category assignments.
  • An Is_Outliers column was created using the IQR method; 15 transactions above the $382.93 upper bound were flagged as extreme-spend transactions.
What this dataset can answer: Which branches generate the most revenue? Which products and categories sell the most? Do members spend differently from normal customers? How do purchasing patterns differ by gender? Are there unusually large transactions?

What it cannot answer well: Because there is no transaction date/time field, it cannot reliably support monthly trends, seasonality, forecasting or year-over-year growth analysis. The presentation also notes that customer history, promotions, inventory levels and richer demographics are unavailable.

Dashboard

The Excel dashboard brings the analysis together through revenue, order, quantity, customer, category, branch, gender and outlier views.

Retail Sales Analytics Dashboard — Excel

Key Results

  • Total revenue: $118,583.90
  • Total transactions: 1,000
  • Total quantity sold: 10,337 units
  • Highest branch revenue: Branch C — $42,584.71
  • Member revenue: $63,213.63
  • Extreme-spend transactions: 1.5%

Business Recommendations

The presentation recommends prioritising high-performing categories, strengthening member-focused strategies, investigating branch differences, monitoring extreme transactions and using the findings to support inventory and promotional decisions.

Limitation

The presentation notes that the dataset did not contain a date/time field, limiting time-series and seasonal analysis.

02

Logistics & Supply Chain Analytics

Completed 31 August 2026

MySQL • SQL • Power BI • Supply Chain Analytics

Project Type

Individual analytics project completed by Chidi Onochie Ronald.

Business Objective

The project investigated logistics costs, inventory availability, supplier performance and operational efficiency to identify areas where the business could reduce avoidable costs, improve replenishment and strengthen supplier decisions.

Data & SQL Analysis

MySQL was used for data cleaning, validation, exploration and SQL-based analysis. The analysis focused on logistics costs, low-stock and high-demand products, supplier performance, transportation and regional cost patterns.

Understanding the Dataset

What this data represents: This is a supply-chain logistics dataset designed to connect product demand, inventory, suppliers, manufacturing, transportation, shipping, quality and logistics costs. The SQL project uses the table supply_chain_data and analyses delivery performance, shipping costs, supplier performance, inventory movement, regional logistics efficiency and lead time.

How to read one row: A row is an operational supply-chain record linking a product/SKU to demand and stock information, a supplier, manufacturing and quality information, and transportation/logistics information. This is why the same dataset can answer inventory, supplier and logistics questions.

Product & Inventory

SKU, Product Type, Price, Units Sold, Stock Levels, Order Quantities and Availability.

Supplier & Quality

Supplier, supplier location, manufacturing cost, production volume, inspection result and defect rate.

Logistics & Delivery

Shipping time, shipping carrier, shipping cost, transportation mode, route, logistics cost and lead-time measures.

How the raw data was prepared in MySQL

  • The CSV was imported into MySQL Workbench and analysed in supply_chain_data.
  • Raw column names were renamed and given appropriate data types, such as LogisticsCosts, OrderLeadTimeDays, SupplierLeadTimeDays, RevenueGenerated and StockLevels.
  • NULL values were checked across key fields, including SKU, revenue, stock, supplier, shipping and lead-time fields.
  • Duplicate SKUs and complete duplicate records were checked.
  • Blank values, leading/trailing spaces and inconsistent categorical text were checked and standardised.
  • Business fields were then used to create replenishment priorities, product-type summaries, supplier comparisons, transportation analysis and regional logistics analysis.
How to understand the business logic: The dataset lets us connect demand + stock to replenishment risk; supplier + cost + quality + lead time to supplier performance; and transport mode + route + carrier + cost + time to logistics efficiency. For example, the analysis identified 26 low-stock SKUs and 1 out-of-stock product, with 13 low-stock SKUs already having sold at least 500 units. It also found a large regional logistics-cost gap: Chennai averaged 621.75 versus 428.34 in Mumbai.
Important limitations: There is no transaction date for time-series or seasonal analysis, no customer ID for customer-level profitability/lifetime-value analysis, no profit field because revenue is available without COGS/profit, and no single authoritative fulfillment-time field. These limits define what the analysis can legitimately conclude.

Power BI Dashboard

The Power BI reporting was organised into three views: Supply Chain Analytics, Inventory & Product Analysis, and Supplier & Logistics Performance.

Key Insights

  • Inventory: 26 SKUs were identified as low-stock and requiring replenishment attention.
  • Supplier performance: Supplier 4 was identified for performance review based on the analysis.
  • Regional costs: Chennai and Bangalore were highlighted for further investigation of logistics costs.
  • Supplier decisions: The analysis supports evaluating suppliers across cost, speed and quality rather than using a single metric.

Business Recommendations

The presentation recommends replenishing the identified low-stock SKUs, reviewing Supplier 4, investigating regional logistics costs, and implementing a supplier scorecard covering cost, speed and quality.

03

E-Commerce Sales Analytics

Completed 24 September 2026

MySQL • SQL • Power BI • Team Project

Project Type

Three-person team project completed by Chidi, Phoebe and Toyeibat.

Business Objective

The project examined where sales were generated and where the business was actually making or losing profit across customers, products, regions, discounts and shipping.

Data & Workflow

The team used MySQL for data cleaning, validation, exploratory analysis and SQL business questions, then translated the results into Power BI dashboards and KPI reporting.

Understanding the Dataset

What this data represents: This is a Superstore / E-Commerce sales dataset containing order, customer, product, geographic, sales, discount, profit and shipping information. After cleaning and validation, the final analytical dataset contained 10,192 records, 20 original analytical fields and 2 derived fields.

How to read one row: The data is analysed at order-line level rather than simply treating every row as a unique order. That is why the final dataset has 10,192 records but only 5,111 distinct orders and 804 distinct customers. A record connects an order to a customer, product, geography, sales value, discount, profit and shipping information.

Commercial

Sales, Quantity, Discount and Profit — used to distinguish sales growth from profitable growth.

Customer & Product

Customer ID/Name, Segment, Product ID/Name, Category and Sub-Category.

Geography & Shipping

Region, State, City, Ship Mode, Order Date and Ship Date.

What was done to make the data analysis-ready?

  • Imported the CSV into MySQL and performed cleaning, validation and transformation in the analysis table.
  • Removed 2 duplicate records.
  • Validated dates, numeric fields, text fields and categorical values.
  • Checked for non-positive sales/quantity, invalid discounts, invalid shipping dates and missing profit values.
  • Removed the technical Row_ID from the final business-analysis table.
  • Created Shipping_Days as Ship_Date minus Order_Date.
  • Created Profit_Margin as Profit divided by Sales × 100.
  • Created year, quarter, month and discount-level groupings for analysis.
Why this dataset supports deeper analysis: Because sales and profit exist together, we can ask not only "What sells?" but also "What is profitable?". That distinction led to findings such as Technology leading category sales and profit, Furniture showing weak profitability, and higher discount levels being associated with weaker margins in the observed dataset.
Power BI model: The project also went beyond a flat dashboard. You created an additional table and connected it to the model using a one-to-many relationship, then used DAX measures/calculations to support the reporting. This demonstrates data modelling as well as visualization.
Important limitations: Shipping_Days measures Order_Date to Ship_Date, not the complete customer delivery cycle. The dataset covers a defined historical period and should not be treated as a forecast. The observed discount/profit relationship is an association in this dataset, not proof that discounting alone caused the profit changes.

Power BI Dashboard

The dashboard contains four pages: Executive Overview; Product & Customer Performance; Profitability & Discount; and Regional & Shipping Analysis.

Key Findings

  • 2026 was identified as the strongest year in the analysis.
  • Technology led category-level profit performance.
  • Furniture showed relatively weaker profitability.
  • High-discount transactions were associated with negative profit.
  • Regional margins varied, highlighting differences in profitability by geography.

Business Recommendations

The presentation recommends using discount thresholds, reviewing weak-margin products, monitoring regional profitability and using customer, product and shipping insights to support more informed commercial decisions.
